# Teaching Summaries: *Paṭicca Samuppāda Dhammaya*

Beginner-friendly summaries of Ven. Kaṭukurunde Ñāṇananda's 20 Pahan Kanuwa sermons on dependent arising (sermons 183–202). They are meant to be read **before** the full book, so that you meet the key concepts first. They follow the six-part structure of the [study plan](../STUDY_PLAN.md).

| Part | Sermons | Summary | Core ideas |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | S1–S4 | [Foundations: the Principle and the Whirlpool](01_Foundations.md) | idappaccayatā; consciousness ⇄ name-and-form; five fingers; contact as root; all links here and now |
| II | S5–S8 | [How the Whirlpool Builds a "World"](02_Building_a_World.md) | language; Kaccānagotta Middle; thisness/otherwiseness; Bāhiya; conceiving; Nibbāna = cessation of six sense-bases |
| III | S9–S12 | [The Middle in Action](03_The_Middle_in_Action.md) | craving the seamstress; kamma and two right views; Poṭṭhapāda training; four floods and seven methods |
| IV | S13–S16 | [The Machinery Up Close](04_The_Machinery_Up_Close.md) | two meanings of saṅkhāra; the All; contact and speed of return; feeling and working with pain |
| V | S17–S19 | [The Far End of the Chain](05_The_Far_End_of_the_Chain.md) | craving as mother; rebirth without a soul; grasping and existence; the unborn; four nutriments; painter vs house with no wall |
| VI | S20 | [Capstone](06_Capstone.md) | the solution in the present; critique of the three-lives model; hetu/paccaya; the five "worlds"; wise attention |

## How to use them

Each summary contains:
- **Key terms**: a short glossary for that part.
- **Core teachings**: each with what the suttas say, the book's similes, and a one-line takeaway.
- **Common misunderstandings**: a table of mistakes and corrections.
- **Self-check questions**, with answers hidden under a click.
- **A one-page recap** and **a practice task**.

Suggested rhythm: read one summary, do its practice for a week, then read the matching sermons in the book.

> **Note:** these summaries present the author's interpretation, which in several places (the three-lives model, the meaning of *nāma*, the intermediate existence) differs from the standard Theravāda commentaries. Check the suttas cited and judge for yourself.
