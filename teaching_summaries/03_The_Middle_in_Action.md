# Part III: The Middle in Action

*Teaching summary of Sermons 9–12 (pp. 217–327)*

> **Builds on Parts I–II:** the whirlpool, the five fingers, the Middle between "is" and "is not", Nibbāna as cessation of the six sense-bases.
> **Part III answers:** How do we *practise* the Middle? What about kamma and rebirth? How does training actually make things cease?

These four sermons coincide with major festivals: **S9 Vesak, S10 Poson, S11 Esala**. They turn the theory of Parts I–II toward **ethics, meditation and the gradual path**.

## Core teaching 1: Two ends and the middle, and craving the seamstress (S9)

### The verse (Tissa Metteyya, Pārāyana)
> *Yo ubhante viditvāna, majjhe mantā na lippati;*
> *taṃ brūmi mahāpurisoti, sodha sibbanimaccagā.*
> "Who, knowing both ends, **does not cling to the middle** by wisdom, him I call a great man; **he has gone beyond the seamstress here**."

### The monks' symposium (Majjhe Sutta, AN 6.61)
After their alms-round at Isipatana, a group of elder monks discussed this verse: *What is the first end? The second? The middle? Who is the seamstress?* **Six interpretations** were offered. In every one, **the seamstress is craving**:

| # | First end | Second end | Middle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **contact** | arising of contact | **cessation of contact** |
| 2 | past | future | present |
| 3 | pleasant feeling | painful feeling | neutral feeling |
| 4 | name | form | consciousness |
| 5 | six internal bases | six external bases | consciousness |
| 6 | identity (5 aggregates) | arising of identity | cessation of identity |

They took the question to the Buddha. He said **all six are well spoken**, but **the first was his intended meaning**. Each is a meditation subject leading to arahantship.

**Note interpretation 4:** consciousness sits **between** name and form. That proves consciousness cannot be part of *nāma* (as Part I argued).

### The sewing-machine simile
Craving is described as:
- *ponobhavikā*: making for renewed existence;
- *nandirāgasahagatā*: bound up with delight and lust;
- *tatratatrābhinandinī*: delighting **here and there**.

**A sewing machine:** each time the needle goes down, the **hidden lower thread** loops around it and ties a knot. You see only the top stitches. Craving is that lower thread, **tying the two ends together while we ignore the middle**.

A **machine with no lower thread** still goes up and down, and the cloths still *seem* joined, **but there is no stitch**. That is the arahant's life: the senses still work, but nothing is tied (the **fruition attainment**, *phala-samāpatti*).

### Who touches? (Moliya Phagguna)
Moliya asked: "*Who touches? Who feels?*" The Buddha: "***Not a proper question.*** *Ask instead: 'Conditioned by what is contact?' Conditioned by the six sense-bases.*" There is **no toucher**, only conditions.

### Adhimutta and the bandits
Captured by bandits, the monk Adhimutta said calmly:
> "*I don't think 'I was', nor 'I will be'. Formations will cease, so why lament? For one who sees **the pure arising of things, the pure series of formations**, as they are, there is no fear.*"

The bandits were so moved they went forth.

### The insight knowledges
As insight deepens: seeing **rise and fall** → seeing **dissolution only** → **fear** (everything is crumbling) → **disenchantment** → **release**.

**Simile:** walking along a ridge with chasms on both sides (past and future), **holding a fence** (formations), and then the fence itself crumbles. Or a **swordsman without a shield**: his sword must both attack and defend; he survives only by speed of attention.

### Takeaway
> **Craving stitches two "ends" together and ignores the middle. Six ways to see this, one of which the Buddha preferred: contact / its arising / its cessation.**

## Core teaching 2: Kamma, its source and its end (S10)

### The three roots
"*Kamma born of greed, hate or delusion ripens wherever one's selfhood comes to be: in this life, the next, or later*" (Nidāna Sutta). It is like **seed in a good field with rain** (rain = craving). When greed etc. are abandoned, that kamma is **like a palm tree cut at the root**, or **a seed burnt to ash and scattered in the wind**.

### Six things to know about kamma (Nibbedhika Sutta)
1. **What is kamma?** "***Cetanāhaṃ bhikkhave kammaṃ vadāmi***": "*Intention, monks, is what I call kamma.*" (The middle finger.)
2. **Its source:** **contact**.
3. **Its diversity:** leading to hell, animal, ghost, human or deva.
4. **Its result:** in this life, the next, or later.
5. **Its cessation:** **the cessation of contact**.
6. **The path:** the Noble Eightfold Path.

The Jains said **bodily action** is the weightiest. The Buddha said **mental action** is (Upāli Sutta): "*Mind is the forerunner of all things.*"

### Kamma is real: two right views
After challenging the commentarial model, the author **firmly keeps kamma and rebirth**. He distinguishes two right views:

| | **Mundane right view** (kammassakatā) | **Supramundane right view** |
|---|---|---|
| Content | Giving has fruit; good and bad deeds have results; **there is this world and the next**; mother and father; spontaneously reborn beings; recluses who know | The **Four Noble Truths** |
| Function | Keeps beings out of bad destinations | Takes beings **across** saṃsāra |

**Don't mix them, and don't throw the first away.** "Rationalists" who say "*there is no next world, no mother, no father*" hold **wrong view leading to hell**. The **Devadūta Sutta** (preached by Mahinda in Anurādhapura) describes the hells vividly. The **Cūḷakammavibhaṅga Sutta**: "*Beings are owners of their kamma, heirs of their kamma … kamma divides beings into inferior and superior.*"

### The critique of the three-lives model
The Visuddhimagga places **ignorance and formations in the past life** and reads **formations as past kamma** that produces **"rebirth-linking consciousness"** in this life. The author replies from the **Vibhaṅga Sutta** (SN 12.2), where the Buddha himself defines the links:

| Link | Buddha's definition (Vibhaṅga Sutta) | Commentarial reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Formations** | **bodily formation** (in-and-out breath), **verbal formation** (applied and sustained thought), **mental formation** (perception and feeling) | past kamma |
| **Consciousness** | the **six** kinds (eye … mind) | rebirth-linking consciousness |

So all twelve links can be seen in the **present**. Ignorance = not knowing the consciousness ⇄ name-and-form interplay; formations = the activity that comes from it (like Narcissus reaching for the water).

### Three kinds of volitional formation
**Merit-making, demerit-making, imperturbable** (formless jhāna): **all born of ignorance**. That is why the Bodhisatta left Āḷāra and Uddaka: even the highest meditative states are not liberation.

### Old kamma and new kamma
The **eye, ear … mind are "old kamma"**: "*constructed, intended, to be felt*". What we do now is new kamma. The **cessation of kamma** is the "*cessation of body, speech and mind*" in the sense of the Sañcetanā Sutta: with the cessation of ignorance, "**that body does not exist, that speech does not exist, that mind does not exist**, … that field, site, base, ground does not exist" for inner pleasure and pain.

### Kusala vs puñña
- **Puñña** (merit) gives pleasant results within saṃsāra. There are ten merit-making acts.
- **Kusala** (skill, wholesomeness) is **linked to wisdom**. There are "*kusala-mūla*" (roots of the wholesome) but **no "puñña-mūla"**. The ten *kusala kammapatha* are mostly **abstentions**, which take hard work.
- The **highest kusala goes beyond both merit and demerit** (*puññapāpapahīna*).

### Takeaway
> **Kamma = intention, sourced in contact, ended by the cessation of contact. Keep mundane right view (kamma, rebirth). Add supramundane right view (Four Truths). Seeing all twelve links in the present does *not* deny rebirth.**

## Core teaching 3: Training makes perceptions cease (S11, Poṭṭhapāda)

### The debate
The wanderer Poṭṭhapāda reports a debate: **how does "higher perception" cease?** Some say without cause; some say perception is a self that comes and goes; some say ascetics or gods put it in and take it out. Background: the yogis of the time were stuck on a **seesaw**: "*perception is a disease; no-perception is delusion; neither-perception-nor-non-perception is peaceful*".

### The Buddha's answer (the refrain)
> "*Perceptions arise and cease **with a cause, with conditions**. **Through training, some perceptions arise; through training, some perceptions cease.***"

### The staircase
Through the **gradual training** (faith → going forth → virtue → sense-restraint → mindfulness → contentment → abandoning the five hindrances → joy → rapture → calm → happiness → concentration):

| Stage | Perception that arises | Perception that ceases |
|---|---|---|
| 1st jhāna | subtle, true perception of rapture-and-happiness born of seclusion | sensual perception |
| 2nd jhāna | … born of concentration | that of the 1st |
| 3rd jhāna | … of equanimous happiness | that of the 2nd |
| 4th jhāna | … of neither-pain-nor-pleasure | that of the 3rd |
| infinite space → infinite consciousness → nothingness | each finer perception | the coarser one |

At **nothingness**, the **peak of perception** (*saññagga*), the meditator reflects:

> "*Intending is worse for me; not intending is better. **What if I neither intend nor form?***"

He does neither, those perceptions cease, and he **touches cessation**. This is the **"gradual cessation of higher perception with full awareness"**. **Dependent arising works like a staircase**: training is conditionality in action.

### Is perception the self?
The Buddha asks Poṭṭhapāda: "*What do you take as self?*" A gross body? A mind-made body? A formless self? In every case, "*one perception arises, another ceases*". So **perception is not self**. The ten unanswered questions (is the world eternal? is the Tathāgata after death? …) are set aside. What the Buddha **does** teach definitely is the **Four Noble Truths**.

### Milk → curd → butter → ghee
Citta Hatthisāriputta asks: *when one "self" (gross, mind-made, formless) is present, are the others false?* The Buddha:

> From the cow comes **milk**; from milk **curd**; from curd **butter**; from butter **ghee**; from ghee **cream of ghee**. **When it is milk, it is called "milk", not curd or ghee.**
> "*These are **the world's designations, expressions, usages, concepts**, which the Tathāgata uses **without grasping** them.*"

The same applies to **past ("I was"), future ("I will be"), present ("I am")**: three pathways of language that must not be mixed (Niruttipatha Sutta). Linguists have praised this simile.

**Unsubstantiated claims:** a man who says he loves "the most beautiful woman in the land" but doesn't know her name, caste or village; or a man building a **ladder to a palace** he has never seen. That is what claims of "an eternally happy self after death" are like.

### Takeaway
> **Perceptions arise and cease through training: the jhānas are a staircase of conditionality. "Self" words are conventions, valid at their moment, like "milk" or "ghee", and are used without grasping.**

## Core teaching 4: Crossing the four floods (S12)

### The paradox (Oghataraṇa Sutta, SN 1.1)
A deity asks the Buddha how he crossed the flood:
> "***When I stood still, I sank; when I struggled, I was swept away. Not standing still, not struggling, I crossed the flood.***"

This is the Middle as **a way of moving**.

### The four floods

| Flood | "Standing still" (sinking) | "Struggling" (swept away) | The middle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sensuality** | hedonists ("*live happily while you live; borrow and drink ghee*") | Jain austerities (burning off old kamma by self-torture) | The Bodhisatta remembered the **first jhāna under the rose-apple tree**: "*why fear a pleasure apart from sensuality?*" → the **Eightfold Path**; use requisites with reflection |
| **Existence** | eternalists **hold back** (fear the cessation of existence) | annihilationists **overshoot** (crave non-existence) | "*Those with vision see **what has come to be as what has come to be***" (Itivuttaka). Simile: one man chases his shadow, another runs from it; the wise see it **as a shadow** |
| **Views** | "everything exists" | "nothing exists" | **Kaccānagotta**: dependent arising (Part II) |
| **Ignorance** | where does ignorance come from? | | **Sammādiṭṭhi Sutta**: "*With the arising of the influxes, ignorance arises; with their cessation, ignorance ceases.*" |

The **Mahācattārīsaka Sutta** shows the Eightfold Path itself as a conditioned sequence: right view → right intention → … → right knowledge → right liberation.

### The Sabbāsava Sutta's seven methods (practical toolkit)
"*I say the destruction of the influxes is for one who knows and sees*": who knows **wise and unwise attention**.

| Method | What it means |
|---|---|
| 1. **Seeing** | Drop the **16 doubts** (Part I); see the Four Truths → stream-entry (abandoning identity view, doubt, clinging to rites) |
| 2. **Restraining** | Guard the six senses |
| 3. **Using** | Use robes, food, shelter, medicine **with reflection** ("*not for fun, not for intoxication …*") |
| 4. **Enduring** | Bear cold, heat, insects, harsh words, pain |
| 5. **Avoiding** | Avoid a wild elephant, a dangerous place, bad friends |
| 6. **Removing** | Dispel thoughts of sensuality, ill-will, cruelty |
| 7. **Developing** | Develop the **seven factors of awakening**, "*dependent on seclusion, dispassion, cessation, ripening in release*" |

**The swimmer** must give way to the current, not fight it. Ānanda: "*The Blessed One has taught the crossing of the flood **by relying on one thing to go to the next***." It is like a raft, used and not grasped.

### Kappa's island
The student Kappa asks for **an island** in the middle of the flood. The Buddha:

> "***Owning nothing, grasping nothing***: *this is the island, there is no other. I call it Nibbāna, the end of decay and death.*"

The author's gloss: there, "**decay itself has decayed, death itself has died**". It is not a lovely holiday island ("Siyadoris Nibbāna").

### Takeaway
> **Neither sink (indulge, hold back) nor struggle (torment, overshoot). Use the seven methods. The "island" is owning nothing and grasping nothing.**

## Common misunderstandings to avoid

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| "If the 12 links are in the present, rebirth isn't real." | The author **keeps** mundane right view: kamma, this world, the next world. |
| "Kamma is fate from past lives." | "*All is due to past action*" is a **rejected** view; kamma = intention, sourced in contact. |
| "Merit is the highest good." | **Kusala** (wisdom-linked skill) is higher; the highest goes **beyond merit and demerit**. |
| "Deep jhāna is liberation." | Imperturbable formations are **still born of ignorance**. Jhāna is a staircase, not the goal. |
| "The middle way = being moderate." | It is a precise **way of moving**: neither stuck nor struggling, and not grasping even the raft. |

## One-page recap

> **Seamstress:** craving ties two ends, ignoring the middle. Six "ends & middle" meditations; the Buddha's own is *contact / arising / cessation*.
>
> **Kamma:** = intention; source = contact; ends with cessation of contact. **Keep mundane right view**; add the Four Truths.
>
> **The three-lives model:** formations = breath / thought / perception-feeling; consciousness = six kinds → all twelve links visible **now**.
>
> **Poṭṭhapāda:** perceptions arise and cease **through training**; jhāna = staircase to the peak of perception → "neither intend nor form" → cessation.
>
> **Language:** milk ≠ curd ≠ ghee; conventions used without grasping.
>
> **Flood:** neither stand still nor struggle; seven methods; island = *owning nothing, grasping nothing*.
