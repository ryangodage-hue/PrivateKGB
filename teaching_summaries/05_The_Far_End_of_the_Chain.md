# Part V: The Far End of the Chain (Craving, Grasping, Existence, Nutriment)

*Teaching summary of Sermons 17–19 (pp. 446–526)*

> **Builds on Parts I–IV:** contact → feeling has been examined closely. Part V follows the chain further: **feeling → craving → grasping → existence → birth**, and then the **four nutriments** that keep the whole process fed.
> **Big question answered here:** *How does rebirth happen without a soul, and how does it stop?*

---

## Core teaching 1: Craving is the mother, ignorance the father (S17)

### Where craving sits
"*Feeling conditions craving; craving conditions grasping.*" The Vibhaṅga Sutta lists **six cravings**, one for each sense object. The first sermon lists **three**: for **sense pleasures**, for **existence** (eternalism: "may my self go on"), for **non-existence** (annihilationism: "may my self be destroyed at death").

### A puzzle
- In the **first sermon** (Dhammacakka), the **origin of suffering = craving**.
- In the **Titthāyatana Sutta**, the **origin of suffering = the twelve links** starting from ignorance.

Is the Buddha contradicting himself? **No.** The author's answer:

> **Craving is the mother; ignorance is the father.** When you mention the mother, you know there is a father somewhere.
> "*Craving gives birth to a person*" (*taṇhā janeti purisaṃ*).

The sequence:
1. In the **darkness of ignorance** there is **groping** (formations).
2. The groping finds **two things**: consciousness and name-and-form.
3. They are really **mutually dependent** (the whirlpool), but the world **takes them as two** ("I" and "world").
4. **From that moment craving operates.** "*He cut off craving here, in name-and-form*" (Samiddhi verse).

So **don't search for a "root cause of ignorance"**, as the commentators did. Ignorance is the background; craving is the active force.

### Recap of the similes of "two"
- The **dog on the plank** wags, growls or stares at its reflection: **greed, hate, delusion**.
- **Narcissus** dies embracing his reflection.
- Ajith and Sumith's **tug-of-war**, the **cricket teams**, the **cinema**: in each, a **two-way relationship** is mistaken for **two separate sides**.

### Where craving arises and hides (Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta)
> "*Where does this craving arise, where does it settle? **Whatever in the world is dear and agreeable**, there it arises, there it settles.*"

Ten groups, for each of the six senses: the internal sense-bases, the external objects, the six consciousnesses, the six contacts, the six feelings, the six perceptions, the six intentions, **the six cravings themselves**, the six applied thoughts, the six sustained thoughts.

**Craving for craving!** We even **enjoy the wanting itself**. And craving **ceases in exactly the same places** it arises.

### The seamstress again
The Majjhe Sutta's six interpretations (Part III) return here. The **sewing machine's hidden lower thread** = *tatratatrābhinandinī*, "delighting here and there".

### Takeaway
> **Ignorance = father (background darkness). Craving = mother (gives birth to "a person"). It arises and hides in whatever is dear: in the senses, in feeling, even in craving itself.**

## Core teaching 2: Rebirth happens moment by moment (S17)

### "Thisness" turning into "otherwiseness"
The Dvayatānupassanā verse: "*A person with craving as his companion wanders long in saṃsāra, **between thisness and otherwiseness**.*"

**Toy-shop simile:** in a shop full of plastic toys, nothing is special. You **buy one** for your child; now it is "**a thing**" with value. The moment the child **breaks it**, it becomes "otherwise". **A thing starts changing from the moment it becomes a thing.** The mind's act that makes it "a thing" is **conceiving** (*maññanā*).

At his awakening the Buddha exclaimed:
> "*This world, **subject to otherwiseness**, attached to existence, overcome by existence, **delights in existence itself**.*"

### Rebirth (*ponobhava*) is not only at death
"*Ponobhavikā*" (making for renewed existence) **happens every moment**: something becomes "a thing", it changes, we forget it and **grab the next**, and the next. That is craving's unseen work: "*delighting here and there*".

**The deer and the mirage posts:** on a plain dotted with posts, a thirsty deer thinks the water is at the first post, runs there, finds nothing, runs to the next … Craving keeps pushing us forward.

### Two images from the Dhammapada
- **The snared hare:** a hare with its hind leg caught in a snare **leaps far**, is jerked back, leaps another way … However far it jumps, the snare holds. Even a non-returner who reaches the Brahma worlds is pulled back if craving remains. *Correction to the commentary:* "*parisappanti*" means **leaping about**, not just "trembling with fear".
- **The tree:** "*As a tree cut down sprouts again if its roots are unharmed, so suffering springs up again and again **until the latent tendency to craving is rooted out**.*"

### Takeaway
> **Renewed existence is happening right now, each time something becomes "a thing" and craving moves on to the next. Death is just the most visible instance.**

## Core teaching 3: The flame carried by the wind: rebirth without a soul (S17)

### The Kutūhalasālā Sutta
The wanderer Vacchagotta asks: "*Other teachers say where their disciples are reborn, and also their liberated ones. You declare where ordinary disciples are reborn, but of the liberated one you only say: '**He cut off craving, turned back the fetters, and by fully understanding conceit made an end of suffering.**' I'm confused.*"

The Buddha: "*It is right to be confused there. **I declare rebirth for one with grasping, not for one without grasping.***"

- **The fire simile:** a fire burns **with fuel** (*sa-upādāno*), not without fuel. (*Upādāna* also means "fuel".)
- Vacchagotta: "*When a **flame is carried far by the wind**, what is its fuel?*" → "***The wind** is its fuel.*"
- "*When a being lays down this body and **reaches another body**, what is its fuel?*" → "***Craving** is its fuel.*"

### Why "intermediate existence" is a misreading
Later schools used this passage to argue for an **antarābhava**, a waiting state between lives. The author argues:
- Vacchagotta asks how one existence **connects** to another, not about a **gap** between them.
- The text says "*reaches **a certain** body*" (*aññataraṃ kāyaṃ anuppatto*). "A certain one" refers to **something that exists**. A variant reading (*anuppanno*, "not yet arisen") led some to imagine a gap.
- **Conclusion:** at death, **craving itself is the "fuel"** that links one life to the next, like wind carrying a flame across to the next tree.

### Craving as "bhavanetti"
Craving is the **conduit to existence**, like the **overhanging ridge** cut along a rock where rainwater collects and pours off: it always channels us into **some** existence.

> "***The world is lacking, insatiable, a slave to craving***" (Raṭṭhapāla Sutta). Ask a millionaire: something is always lacking.

### Takeaway
> **What links life to life is not a soul but craving, the "fuel", like wind carrying a flame. Where there is no grasping, there is no rebirth to declare.**

## Core teaching 4: "Isn't wanting Nibbāna also craving?" (S17)

A common modern question. Two suttas answer it.

### The nun who feigned illness (Bhikkhunī Sutta)
A nun with **improper intentions** sent for Ānanda, pretending to be ill. Ānanda saw through it and taught:

| "This body is born of … | … and with its help, abandon it" |
|---|---|
| **food** | relying on food, abandon food (eat mindfully, with reflection, until craving for food goes) |
| **craving** | relying on craving, abandon craving ("*So-and-so became an arahant; when will I?*" That longing **powers the effort**) |
| **conceit** | relying on conceit, abandon conceit ("*If he can, why can't I?*") |
| **sexual intercourse** | **here the Buddha taught "breaking the bridge"**, *not* "relying on it". |

The nun got up and asked forgiveness. (The author notes that some later Tantric schools misread this to justify the opposite of what it says.)

### Uṇṇābha's question
The brahmin Uṇṇābha asks Ānanda: "*You practise to abandon desire, by developing **desire**-concentration (the four bases of success)? That's impossible: desire removing desire!*"

Ānanda: "*Before you came to this park, you had the **desire** to come, the **energy**, the **intention**, the **investigation** of the route. **Now that you've arrived, have they subsided?***" "*Yes.*"

**Worldly goals breed new goals.** The desire for Nibbāna, **"the destruction of craving"** (*taṇhakkhaya*), **ends on arrival**. This is the Middle again: not the Jains' refusal of food and clothing, but **using with reflection**.

### Hooks turned the other way
"*He turned back the fetters*" (*vāvattayi saṃyojanaṃ*): like **railway carriages** coupled by hooks. Turn the hooks the other way and the carriages are no longer linked. The last and subtlest coupling is **the conceit "I am"** (*asmimāna*), which even a non-returner still has.

### Takeaway
> **Wholesome desire for the goal is used like a raft and ends on arrival. Worldly desire always breeds more. Rely on craving to abandon craving.**

## Core teaching 5: Grasping → existence → birth (S18)

### The verse
> "*Grasping conditions existence; **one who has come to be meets suffering**; for the born there is death: this is the arising of suffering. Therefore, with the destruction of grasping, the wise, rightly understanding, **directly knowing the destruction of birth**, do not come to renewed existence.*"

### What is grasping?
Four kinds: **sensuality, views, rites and rituals, self-theories**. *Upa-ādāna* = **drawing near and holding** (with the mind, not the hand).

In the Mahāpuṇṇama Sutta a monk asks: "*Is grasping the same as the five aggregates, or something apart from them?*" The Buddha:
> "*Neither. **Whatever desire-and-lust there is for the five aggregates: that is the grasping there.***"

### What is existence? (Bhava Sutta)
Ānanda: "*How far is there 'existence'?*" The Buddha:
> "***Kamma is the field, consciousness the seed, craving the moisture.*** *For beings hindered by ignorance and fettered by craving, consciousness becomes established in a sense-realm, form-realm or formless realm.*"

### Birth and death come as a pair
"*One who has come to be (*bhūta*) meets suffering*": existence **includes** birth, and birth **includes** decay and death. Every teacher tried to keep **birth without death** (an eternal life). Salla Sutta: "*There is no means by which the born do not die.*"

So **if you don't want death, you must end birth**. And birth ends when **grasping ends**.

### Takeaway
> **Grasping = desire-and-lust for the aggregates. Field (kamma) + seed (consciousness) + water (craving) = existence. Existence includes birth, and birth includes death.**

## Core teaching 6: Suffering as a starting point: the Upanisa Sutta (S18)

### The "transcendental" dependent arising
Most presentations of dependent arising go forward to suffering, then backward to cessation. The **Upanisa Sutta** keeps **going forward past suffering**:

> ignorance → formations → … → birth → **suffering** → **faith** → gladness → rapture → tranquillity → happiness → concentration → knowledge-and-vision of things as they are → disenchantment → dispassion → liberation → **knowledge of destruction (of the influxes)**

**Simile:** rain on a mountain-top fills the crevices, then the pools, the lakes, the streams, the rivers, the **ocean**.

### Why "upanisā", not "paccaya"
The Sinhala translations render *upanisā* as *paccaya* (condition), which **hides an important point**:
- **Paccaya** is **invariable**: "*birth conditions death*", whether Buddhas arise or not.
- **Upanisā** means a **supporting condition**: things "*lean on*" it. It is **optional**.

### Suffering has two possible results (Nibbedhika Sutta)
> "*I say suffering results either in **bewilderment** or in **a search**: 'Who knows a word or two to end this suffering?'*"

- **Paṭācārā** lost husband, children, parents and went mad. A kind stranger pointed her to the Buddha, and she became an arahant.
- **Kisāgotamī** carried her dead child, looking for medicine. She too was sent to the Buddha and became an arahant.

For most of saṃsāra, suffering has led only to **bewilderment**, because there was no Buddha, no good friend (*kalyāṇamitta*). **Now there is.** That is why this chance is precious.

### Takeaway
> **Suffering can lead to bewilderment or to a search. With a good friend, the search becomes faith → joy → concentration → wisdom → liberation.**

## Core teaching 7: "There is an unborn" means freedom here and now (S18)

### The Ajāta Sutta (Itivuttaka)
> "*There is, monks, an **unborn, unbecome, unmade, unconditioned**. If there were not, no escape from the born, become, made, conditioned would be known **here**. But because there is, an escape is known.*"

Many read this as describing **a separate eternal realm**. The author reads it as **the cessation of existence (bhava-nirodha) experienced by arahants in the fruition attainment**. As existence comes through the six sense-bases, the **cessation of the six sense-bases is the cessation of existence**.

The verses that follow:
> "*The born, become, arisen, made, conditioned, unstable, bound to decay-and-death, a nest of disease, perishing, **arising from nutriment and the conduit (craving)**: not fit to delight in. The escape from it: **peaceful, beyond reasoning, stable, unborn, unarisen, sorrowless, stainless**, the cessation of suffering-things, **the stilling of formations, bliss**.*"

> ⚠️ **Warning:** don't turn the "unconditioned" into **another world** (that is again "Siyadoris Nibbāna"). This is the same as **non-manifesting consciousness**, in which name-and-form no longer appears.

### Why it can't be grasped by logic
**Mahānidāna**: "*So far is the pathway of language … so far the range of wisdom … so far the round goes round: **name-and-form together with consciousness**.*"
- **Language, logic, even wisdom operate only between consciousness and name-and-form.**
- Once that bond is broken, there is something **"beyond reasoning"** (*atakkāvacara*).

That is why the Buddha set aside the **four-cornered questions** about the Tathāgata after death (is / is not / both / neither). They assume a self. "*The Tathāgata, **freed from reckoning in terms of form**, is deep, immeasurable, hard to fathom, like the ocean.*"

### The sage is "not reckoned" (Muni Sutta)
> "*Having **assessed the fields, measured the seed**, he does not water it with moisture. That sage, **seeing the end of birth**, giving up logic, **does not come into reckoning**.*"

The fields = kamma; the seed = consciousness; the moisture = craving (Bhava Sutta). The arahant still lives, **but doesn't water the seed**.

"Not reckoned" also has an everyday sense: the world "*counts*" people by their **possessions** (land, office, vehicles). Arahants have **put down the five aggregates**, so the world doesn't "count" them.

### "Parinibbuta" in this life
The Cūḷasaccaka Sutta says of the Buddha:
> "*Crossed over, the Blessed One teaches for crossing. Calmed, he teaches for calm. Tamed, he teaches for taming. **Fully quenched (parinibbuto), he teaches for full quenching.***"

If "parinibbuta" meant "dead", how could he *teach*? So **parinibbāna is realised in life**. The commonly heard "*grasping conditions existence; without grasping, parinibbāna*" means the same. The author's argument: if you could not **know in this life** that there is no more birth, **doubt** (= ignorance) would remain. Hence "***birth is destroyed***" (*khīṇā jāti*) is **directly known**.

### Takeaway
> **The "unborn" is the cessation of existence, known here and now. Logic works only inside the consciousness ⇄ name-and-form circle; the freed one is "beyond reckoning".**

## Core teaching 8: The four nutriments (S19)

### What keeps the process going?
> "*Whatever suffering arises, all is due to **nutriment**. With the cessation of nutriment, there is no arising of suffering.*" (Dvayatānupassanā)

Āhāra Sutta: "*There are four nutriments for beings that have come to be, or for the support of those seeking birth: **edible food**, gross or subtle; **contact**; **mental intention**; **consciousness**. All four have **craving** as their source.*"

### The Puttamaṃsa Sutta: four shocking similes

| Nutriment | Simile | What full understanding brings |
|---|---|---|
| **Edible food** | A couple crossing a desert run out of food. To survive, they **kill their only, beloved son**, and eat his flesh, beating their breasts: "*Where is our only son?*" Did they eat for fun, strength or beauty? No, **only to cross the desert**. | Understanding food → understanding **lust for the five sense-pleasures** → non-returner |
| **Contact** | A **flayed cow** (patches of skin gone): wherever it stands (by a wall, under a tree, in water, in the open), creatures bite its wounds | Understanding contact → understanding the **three feelings** → arahant |
| **Mental intention** | A man who wants to live is **dragged by two strong men toward a pit of glowing coals**. His wish, longing and aim is to be far from it | Understanding intention → understanding the **three cravings** → arahant |
| **Consciousness** | A robber is **struck with 100 spears** in the morning, 100 at noon, 100 in the evening | Understanding consciousness → understanding **name-and-form** → arahant |

### Notes
- **The commentary blunts the shock.** It adds a sentimental story: the parents argue over who should die and **the child dies by itself**. The author says this **destroys the Buddha's point**, and that this softened version is what most Sinhala readers know.
- **It is not about meat versus vegetarian.** The simile is about **all food**. "*If a grain of rice could speak, it would tell of the blood and tears in which it was produced*". Today, with farm chemicals killing countless small creatures, it would weep.
- **Modern "spear strokes":** TV, advertising and neon signs ("*look at us!*") are today's **"ad-strokes"** on consciousness. We don't even recognise them as suffering.
- **The charcoal pit today:** lying awake at night worrying about a debt, thoughts **dragged** back and forth; the deer running to the mirage.

### The middle way with food
Not starvation (the Bodhisatta tried that) and not indulgence:
1. **Reflection** at each meal: "*Wisely reflecting, I use this food **not for fun, not for intoxication, not for beauty, not for decoration**, but only to sustain this body, to end hunger, and to support the holy life; I'll end the old feeling (hunger) and not create a new one (over-eating).*"
2. **Element reflection:** "*This alms-food is **mere elements**; the one who eats it is **mere elements, not a being, not a soul, empty**.*"
3. **Repulsiveness:** clean food becomes **repulsive** once it enters "*this foul body*".

**Similes:** like **honey on a razor**; like **ointment on a wound**; like **grease on an axle** (to carry the load of the five aggregates across the desert of saṃsāra). Sāriputta: "*Stop four or five mouthfuls short and drink water.*" The first of the novice Sopāka's ten questions: "***All beings subsist on nutriment.***"

### Takeaway
> **Four nutriments, all rooted in craving: food, contact, intention, consciousness. Understanding each fully (desert, flayed cow, coal pit, 300 spears) leads to non-return or arahantship.**

## Core teaching 9: The painter and the house with no wall (S19)

This is **the clearest picture of rebirth and its ending** in the whole book.

### With lust: the painter (Atthirāga Sutta)
> "*If there is **lust, delight, craving** for any of the four nutriments, consciousness **becomes established there and grows**. Where consciousness is established and grows, **name-and-form descends**. Where name-and-form descends, **formations grow**. Where formations grow, there is **renewed existence in the future** … birth, decay and death.*"

> "*Just as a **dyer or painter**, with dye or lac, turmeric, indigo or crimson, would **create the figure of a man or woman, complete in all its parts**, on a well-polished plank, a wall or a cloth …*"

- Lust, delight and craving are **the paint**. Consciousness is **the surface**. Name-and-form is **the picture** that appears on it.
- The embryo in the womb, the "mud stain" (*kalala*), **already carries the figure** of the being to be born.
- **We don't decide to paint.** While lust remains, the picture **paints itself**.
- (One addicted to food may be reborn as a worm or a pig in that very food.)

### Without lust: the house with no wall
> "*Suppose there is a **peaked house** with windows on the north, south and east. When the sun rises, **where does the light land**?*" "*On the western wall.*"
> "*If there were no western wall?*" "*On the ground.*" "*No ground?*" "*On the water.*" "*No water?*"
> "***Then it would not land anywhere.***" (*appatiṭṭhita*)

**Without lust, consciousness finds no footing:** it is **unestablished**. No name-and-form "descends", no renewed existence.

### Painting on empty space (Kakacūpama Sutta)
A man says, "*I'll paint a picture **on the sky** with these colours.*" Impossible: "***Space is formless and non-manifesting (*anidassano*)***." That is the meaning of **anidassana viññāṇa**: **non-manifesting consciousness**, which shows no name-and-form.

> "*Consciousness that is **non-manifesting, infinite, all-radiant**: here earth, water, fire and air find no footing; here long and short, fine and coarse, pleasant and unpleasant; **here name-and-form cease without remainder**. With the cessation of consciousness, all this ceases.*" (Kevaddha Sutta)

Read with the painter simile: **no paint (lust) → no picture (name-and-form) → nothing manifests**. "Cessation of consciousness" here means cessation of **the ordinary, "made" consciousness that goes with name-and-form**.

### The track of birds in the sky
> "*Those who have no hoard, who have **fully understood food**, whose pasture is **emptiness and signless liberation**: their track is hard to trace, **like that of birds in the sky**.*" (Dhammapada, Arahanta Vagga)

The Buddha "*experiences taste but not lust for taste*" (Brahmāyu Sutta). Arahants eat, but **food is not their "pasture"**: **emptiness** is.

### Takeaway
> **With lust, consciousness lands and name-and-form is "painted" on it: that is rebirth. Without lust, like sunlight in a house with no wall, it lands nowhere: that is freedom, here and now and at death.**

## Common misunderstandings to avoid

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| "We must find the root cause of ignorance." | Ignorance is the **background (father)**; craving is the **active force (mother)**. |
| "Rebirth happens only at death." | It happens **moment by moment**; death is the most visible case. |
| "Something waits in an intermediate state between lives." | The author argues: at death **craving is the fuel** linking one life to the next; no gap needed. |
| "Wanting Nibbāna is just another craving." | Wholesome desire is **used to abandon craving and ends on arrival**. |
| "The 'unborn' is a heavenly realm." | It is **the cessation of existence**, experienced. |
| "Parinibbāna = death." | The Buddha **taught as one parinibbuta**. It is realised in life. |
| "The son's-flesh simile is about vegetarianism." | It is about **all nutriment** and **craving**. |

## One-page recap

> **Craving** = mother, **ignorance** = father. Craving hides in all that is dear, even in craving itself.
>
> **Moment-to-moment rebirth:** thisness → otherwiseness → grab the next (toy, hare, mirage posts).
>
> **Flame on the wind:** craving is the fuel between bodies; no soul, and no gap needed.
>
> **Desire for Nibbāna:** use it to end craving; it **ends on arrival** (Uṇṇābha).
>
> **Existence:** kamma = field, consciousness = seed, craving = moisture. Birth and death are a pair; end birth by ending grasping.
>
> **Upanisa:** suffering → bewilderment *or* search → faith → … → liberation.
>
> **The unborn:** the cessation of existence known **here**; beyond logic; parinibbuta **in life**.
>
> **Four nutriments:** son's flesh, flayed cow, coal pit, 300 spears.
>
> **Painter vs house with no wall:** with lust, name-and-form is painted on consciousness; without lust, the light lands nowhere.

**Practice for this part:** Before each meal, recite the food reflection and add: "*mere elements, not a being, empty*." Each evening, note one moment where something became "a thing" and one where it became "otherwise".
